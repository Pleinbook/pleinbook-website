## Pigment mixing

<SearchTerms terms="mixing, blend colours, subtractive, Kubelka Munk, blue and yellow, muddy, realistic paint" />

Most apps blend colours like light, so blue and yellow make grey. Pleinbook mixes them like paint,
so blue and yellow make green, red and white make pink, and red and ultramarine make a deep violet.

Mixing happens everywhere paint meets paint: when strokes overlap, when a Wet brush or your finger
smudges, on the scratchpad, and where paint sits over toned paper. There's nothing to switch on.

## The scratchpad

<SearchTerms terms="palette, mixing area, test strokes, scratch paper, swatches, practise" />

The scratchpad is a separate piece of paper for mixing colours and trying brushes before you touch
your painting. Tap the palette button on the tool rail to show it, and the **×** on its bar to put
it away.

<Screenshot src={ColourScratchpad} alt="The scratchpad in the bottom-left corner, with yellow and blue strokes mixed into green, and the kit along its bar." caption="The scratchpad, with the kit along its bar." />

It works like the page: the same brushes, wet paint and mixing, and no undo. Hold a finger on it to
pick up a colour you've mixed. It always uses the same paper as the page you're painting on.

- **Move it** by dragging its bar, and **resize it** from the corner. The bar can go above or below
  the paper.
- **Your kit** sits at the left of the bar, so you can change brush without leaving it.
- **Run out of space?** Tap **›** to turn to the next scratchpad page, or **+** for a new one.
- **Look back** with the grid button: every scratchpad page you've used, oldest first.

Each painting keeps its scratchpad. Open a page again and the scratchpad page you used with it comes
back, so the mixes for every painting stay with it.

## Picking a colour

<SearchTerms terms="colour picker, color picker, wheel, sliders, HSV, RGB, hex, recent colours" />

Tap the colour disc on the tool rail to open the colour panel. Choose **Wheel** (a hue ring around a
saturation and brightness square) or **Sliders** (H, S, V and R, G, B). Your recent colours sit
along the top.

<Screenshot src={ColourPanelWheel} alt="The colour panel open over the page, showing the hue ring and square, the current colour and recent colours." caption="The colour panel, on Wheel." />

- **Put on scratchpad** lays the colour on the scratchpad as a swatch, ready to mix.
- **Pin** the panel to keep it open while you paint; drag its title to move it, and its corner to
  resize it. Unpinned, it closes when you start painting.
- **Drag the colour disc** up or down without opening the panel to lighten or darken the current
  colour.

The kit ring has the same wheel in its middle, for changing colour without going to the rail.

## The eyedropper

<SearchTerms terms="eyedropper, colour picker, sample, pick colour, loupe, dropper" />

Pick up any colour you see on the page or the scratchpad:

- **Hold a finger still** for a moment. A loupe shows the colour under your finger above the colour
  you have now; lift to take it.
- **Drag from the eyedropper** on the tool rail onto the page and lift.
- **Tap the eyedropper** on the tool rail, and every touch picks a colour until you tap it again.
- **Squeeze Apple Pencil Pro** (the default) and lift to pick where the Pencil is hovering.

A finger takes a small average of the colours under it; the Pencil picks an exact point.

## Palettes

<SearchTerms terms="palettes, swatches, colour sets, import palette, gpl, ase, aco, from photo" />

Tap **Swatches** on the scratchpad's bar to see your recent colours and your palettes. Tap a colour
to lay it on the scratchpad and pick it up, or tap a palette's tube to lay out every colour in it.

<Screenshot src={ColourSwatches} alt="The Swatches popover above the scratchpad, showing recent colours, an empty New Palette bar and Import Palette." caption="Swatches: recent colours, a new palette and your saved palettes." />

- **Make a palette:** tap the empty slots in the New Palette bar to add the current colour, then
  **Save** and name it.
- **Import a palette** from a palette file (.gpl, .kpl, .aco, .ase or .swatches), or take eight
  colours from a photo.
- **Starter palettes** include Zorn, Limited Earth, Split Primary, Apelles and Ultramarine + Burnt
  Sienna. Restore them from **Settings › Resources › Palettes** if you delete them.
