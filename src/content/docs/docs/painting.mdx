## The page

<SearchTerms terms="canvas, zoom, pan, rotate, orientation, fit, screen, layers, one layer" />

A Pleinbook page is one layer of paint on paper, and it's always shown whole. There's no zoom, pan
or rotate: to see the page differently, move the device.

The page is fixed to the glass. Turn the device and the controls turn to stay upright, but the
painting doesn't move. Your brush tips turn with the controls, so they keep their angle to you.

A sketchbook's pages are all one size, set when you [make the sketchbook](/docs/sketchbooks/#making-a-sketchbook).
At the default size, one pixel of the page is one pixel of the screen. A bigger page is scaled down
to fit, never cropped.

Paint never dries. Everything on the page stays wet, so you can keep blending into it for as long as
the page exists.

## Paper

<SearchTerms terms="paper colour, toned paper, grain, texture, tooth, background, cold press, rough, smooth" />

Paint sits on paper: a colour and, optionally, a grain. Each sketchbook has its own paper, and any
page can override it: long-press the page in its sketchbook and choose **Paper…**.

Pick a colour, a grain, and how deep and large the grain is. **Use the sketchbook's paper** removes
the override.

<Screenshot src={PaintingPaper} alt="The Paper for this page sheet: the paper colour, a row of textures with Rough selected, and the Depth and Scale sliders." caption="A page's paper, with the Rough grain." />

Paint mixes with the paper beneath it, so a thin glaze over toned paper looks like a glaze, not a
tint laid on top. Brushes with tooth leave light strokes on the tops of the grain, as real paper
does.

Changing a page's paper replaces it, including any adjustments made to it. Premium papers come with
[Pleinbook Unlock](/docs/pleinbook-unlock/#premium-covers-and-papers).

## Dry, Wet and Lasso brushes

<SearchTerms terms="brush types, engines, stamping, smudge brush, blender, fill tool, paint brush" />

Pleinbook has three kinds of brush:

- **Dry** brushes lay paint down, dab by dab: pencils, inks, gouache, bristle and textured brushes.
- **Wet** brushes pick up the paint already on the page and drag it, mixing as they go. Blenders and
  loaded brushes are Wet.
- **Lasso** brushes fill the shape you draw. See [Lasso fills](#lasso-fills).

All three mix colour as pigment. Every brush can be [taken apart in the brush editor](/docs/brushes/#the-brush-editor),
and you can change a brush's kind there too.

## Erasing

<SearchTerms terms="eraser, rubber, remove paint, delete marks, clear" />

The eraser is a brush like any other: it's in the default kit, and you can leave it out of your
kits if you prefer. It removes paint down to the paper and never removes the paper itself.

Any Dry or Lasso brush can erase: open it in the brush editor and set **Opacity › Blend** to
**Erase**. The Lasso Eraser clears a whole shape at once.

## Lasso fills

<SearchTerms terms="fill, fill tool, shape, lasso, gradient fill, flat fill, bucket" />

Choose a Lasso brush and draw a shape. It fills as you draw, with the closing edge running straight
from your pen back to where you started, and the fill goes down when you lift.

Anything inside the shape is filled, even where your line crosses itself, so you never get holes.

The starter Lasso brushes show what fills can do:

- **Lasso Fill**: a flat, solid fill.
- **Lasso Glaze**: a thin, soft-edged glaze.
- **Lasso Gradient** and **Lasso Glow**: fills that fade, along the line you draw or out from where
  you started.
- **Lasso Pressure**: your pressure along the edge sets how much paint goes down.
- **Lasso Texture**: a fill with texture.
- **Lasso Eraser**: clears the shape.

In the brush editor's **Fill** page, choose **Flat** or **Gradient**, the gradient's direction
(**Along** your line or **Out** from the start) and its curve, and **Edge softness** for a feathered
edge.
