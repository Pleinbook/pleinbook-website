## Your first page

<SearchTerms terms="start, begin, new, first launch, blank page, how to paint" />

Pleinbook opens on a blank page in a sketchbook called **Sketchbook 1**, ready to paint. After that,
it always opens on the last page you had open.

1. **Choose a brush.** Tap the brush button at the top of the tool rail on the right, then tap a
   brush. Round Opaque is a good one to start with.
2. **Choose a colour.** Tap the colour disc on the tool rail and pick from the wheel, or mix your
   own on the [scratchpad](/docs/colour/#the-scratchpad).
3. **Paint.** Strokes go straight onto the page. The page stays wet, so you can keep pushing paint
   around with a [Wet brush](/docs/painting/#dry-wet-and-lasso-brushes) or your finger.
4. **Turn the page.** On the last page of a sketchbook, the arrow in the top bar becomes a **+**. Tap it for a fresh
   page in the same sketchbook.

When a page isn't working, start the next one rather than fixing the last. That's what a
sketchbook is for. The goal isn't to never finish, but rather to not be too precious about sketches.
Sometimes a fresh start is all it takes for the inspiration to flow after what feels like a big blockage.

## The drawing screen

<SearchTerms terms="interface, toolbar, top bar, tool rail, buttons, controls, icons, full screen" />

The page fills the screen, and the controls float over it. While you paint with Apple Pencil, any
panel your stroke comes near fades out of the way, then comes back a moment after you lift.

## Apple Pencil and fingers

<SearchTerms terms="gestures, touch, finger painting, smudge, double tap, squeeze, Pencil Pro, hover, no Pencil" />

The first time Pleinbook sees an Apple Pencil, the Pencil becomes your brush and fingers do other
jobs. You can choose for yourself in **Settings › Painting › Paint with**.

### With Apple Pencil

- **The Pencil paints** with the current brush. Hovering shows the brush's outline before you touch
  down.
- **One finger smudges**, using the Finger Smudge brush. Choose another brush for your finger by
  long-pressing it in the brush library and choosing **Assign to Finger**, or turn the finger off in
  **Settings › Painting › One finger**.
- **Hold a finger still** on the page for a moment to pick up a colour. A loupe shows the colour
  under your finger; lift to take it. Touch down with the Pencil while holding to pick an exact
  point.
- **Double-tap** the Pencil to open the kit ring, and **squeeze** Apple Pencil Pro to pick up the
  colour under the tip. Change either in **Settings › Apple Pencil**.
- Fingers are ignored while the Pencil is touching the glass, so a resting hand doesn't smudge.

### Without a Pencil

- **One finger paints**, with pressure taken from how much of your finger touches the glass.
  Calibrate it in **Settings › Pressure and touch › Finger pressure**.
- **Two fingers smudge.**
- **Hold a finger still** to pick up a colour.

**Three fingers** slide the page within the space around it, when the page doesn't fill the screen.

## No undo, and Easy Mode

<SearchTerms terms="undo, redo, revert, mistake, take back, go back, cancel stroke, Easy Mode" />

We believe in a sketchbook being raw and authentic, so undo is off by default, even if that means
embracing mistakes and accidents. Every mark is a commitment, as it is on paper.

If you'd like a safety net while you learn, turn on **Easy Mode** in Settings. An **Undo** button
appears in the top bar and takes back the page's last change: a stroke, a fill, an adjustment or a
mirror. It's a single step: after you use it, it waits for your next mark.

The scratchpad never has undo, with or without Easy Mode.

## Locking a page

<SearchTerms terms="lock, protect, finished, read only, accidental strokes, padlock" />

When a painting is finished, lock it so a stray stroke can't spoil it. Tap the padlock in the top
bar, or long-press the page in its sketchbook and choose **Lock**.

A locked page can't be painted on, mirrored, adjusted or deleted, and a "Page locked" note appears
if you try. You can still look at it, pick colours from it, export it and use the scratchpad
alongside it.

You can also lock a **whole sketchbook** from its menu in the library. No pages can then be added
to it, moved or changed.

## Saving your painting

<SearchTerms terms="save, export, share, Photos, camera roll, Files, AirDrop, PNG, kra, Krita, send, download" />

Pleinbook keeps your work as you paint, so there's no save button. To take a painting out of
Pleinbook, tap the book in the top bar to see the sketchbook's pages, then long-press the page:

- **Save to Photos** adds it to your photo library as a full-resolution PNG.
- **Share…** sends the same PNG anywhere: **Save to Files**, AirDrop, Messages, Mail or another
  app.
- **Export for Krita…** makes a .kra file, with the paint and the paper on separate layers, and
  opens the same share options.

For a loose page, long-press it in the library instead. More in
[Exporting pages](/docs/import-and-export/#exporting-pages).
